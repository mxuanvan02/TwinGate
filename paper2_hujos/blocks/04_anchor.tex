\section{Device-Anchored Attestation}
\label{sec:anchor}

\subsection{The gateway record}

Proposition~\ref{prop:blind} shows that volume and depth are insufficient, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:coincide} shows that order is exactly what the adversary
manipulates. The defence must therefore observe timestamps --- and the only party
that can vouch for a timestamp is the device that acted on it.

Let the gateway hold a signed command record
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{G}=\{(t_j,u_j,\sigma_j)\},
\label{eq:gateway}
\end{equation}
where $\sigma_j$ is a signature over $(t_j,u_j)$ produced by the controller at
actuation time. The anchor test compares the claimed log against it:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_{\mathrm{time}}&=\{t_i\}\triangle\{t_j\},\\
\Delta_{\mathrm{depth}}&=\max_{t}\bigl|u(t)-u_{\mathcal{G}}(t)\bigr|,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:deltas}
\end{equation}
where $\triangle$ is the symmetric difference and $\Delta_{\mathrm{depth}}$ is
taken over hours common to both records. The verdict is then
\begin{equation}
A(L,\mathcal{G})=
\begin{cases}
\text{forge}, & \Delta_{\mathrm{time}}\neq\emptyset
\ \vee\ \Delta_{\mathrm{depth}}>\epsilon,\\
\text{match}, & \text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\label{eq:anchor}
\end{equation}
with $\epsilon=10^{-6}$\,mm, i.e.\ exact agreement up to floating-point noise.
Naming the two discrepancies separately matters for what follows: time-shifting
alters $\Delta_{\mathrm{time}}$ only, depth inflation alters
$\Delta_{\mathrm{depth}}$ only, and omission and addition alter both, which is
why a single comparison catches all four.
Time-shifting, consolidation, addition, omission and depth inflation all alter
either the hour set or a depth at a common hour, so all are caught.

\begin{proposition}[Completeness of the anchor]
\label{prop:complete}
If a claimed log $L$ differs from the gateway record $\mathcal{G}$ in the
multiset of hours or in any depth at a shared hour, then $A(L,\mathcal{G})$
returns \text{forge}. If $L$ and $\mathcal{G}$ agree on both, then $L$ is a
faithful transcript and no forgery of the three quantities in \eqref{eq:three}
has occurred.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The first clause is the definition of \eqref{eq:anchor}. For the second, total
volume, individual depths and timestamps are all functions of the hour set and
the per-hour depths, so agreement on those two fixes all three.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Why this does not re-introduce false accusation}

Proposition~\ref{prop:complete} is strong enough to look dangerous: any
discrepancy is treated as forgery, including the forgetting and rounding that
Sec.~\ref{sec:guarantee} showed to be ubiquitous. The resolution is that the
anchor does not compare a log against a \emph{model}; it compares a transcript
against the \emph{source}. An honest farmer who forgets an entry is not accused
of fraud, because the system of record is the gateway, not the farmer's memory:
the claim is simply completed from $\mathcal{G}$. In operational terms the anchor
turns the log from \emph{evidence the farmer must get right} into \emph{a view of
a record the device already holds}. False accusation then requires the gateway to
disagree with itself, which is a device-integrity question rather than a
behavioural one, and is why the guarantee in Table~\ref{tab:falseacc} holds at
$0/60$ while the behavioural tiers hold at $26.7\%$ and $5.0\%$.

\subsection{Cost and the scope of the claim}

The anchor requires the gateway of the companion work~\cite{twingate2026}, which
actuates the pump and already writes a signed, time-stamped command record as
part of its safety function. The marginal cost of attestation is therefore the
storage and transmission of a record that exists regardless, which is why the
attested-log row of Table~\ref{tab:baseline} remains near zero.

The scope must nonetheless be stated precisely, because it is narrower than the
premise we started from. A log written by hand on a handset, with no device in
the loop, is \emph{not} admissible evidence on its own: Sec.~\ref{sec:results}
measures a rational adversary inflating credits by $+35\%$ in paired
comparison while escaping every physics-based and volume-based test
($6/6$ altered logs, under both the point model and the robust tier). The honest formulation of the result is that
near-zero-cost MRV is achievable, but only where a controller stands between the
farmer and the pump. Where irrigation is manual --- a sluice gate opened by hand
--- the anchor is unavailable and this layer does not apply.
