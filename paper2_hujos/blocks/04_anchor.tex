\section{Device-Anchored Attestation}
\label{sec:anchor}

\subsection{The gateway record}

Propositions~\ref{prop:blind}--\ref{prop:coincide} show that volume and depth
are insufficient and that order is exactly what the adversary manipulates, so
the defence must observe timestamps --- and the only party that can vouch for
a timestamp is the device that acted on it. Let the gateway hold a signed
command record
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{G}=\{(t_j,u_j,\sigma_j)\},
\label{eq:gateway}
\end{equation}
where:
\begin{symbolist}
\item[$\mathcal{G}$] the gateway's command record, covering every pump actuation in the season;
\item[$t_j$] the hour at which the controller switched the pump for command $j$;
\item[$u_j$] the water depth the controller delivered for that command, in mm;
\item[$\sigma_j$] a digital signature over the pair $(t_j,u_j)$, produced by the controller at actuation time.
\end{symbolist} The anchor test compares the claimed log against it:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_{\mathrm{time}}&=\{t_i\}\triangle\{t_j\},\\
\Delta_{\mathrm{depth}}&=\max_{t}\bigl|u(t)-u_{\mathcal{G}}(t)\bigr|,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:deltas}
\end{equation}
where:
\begin{symbolist}
\item[$\Delta_{\mathrm{time}}$] the set of hours present in one record but absent from the other;
\item[$\triangle$] the symmetric difference of the two hour sets, returning elements belonging to only one of them;
\item[$u(t)$] the depth claimed at hour $t$ in log $L$;
\item[$u_{\mathcal{G}}(t)$] the depth the gateway recorded at the same hour $t$;
\item[$\Delta_{\mathrm{depth}}$] the largest discrepancy between the two depths, taken over hours common to both records.
\end{symbolist}

The verdict is then
\begin{equation}
A(L,\mathcal{G})=
\begin{cases}
\text{forge}, & \Delta_{\mathrm{time}}\neq\emptyset
\ \vee\ \Delta_{\mathrm{depth}}>\epsilon,\\
\text{match}, & \text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\label{eq:anchor}
\end{equation}
where:
\begin{symbolist}
\item[$A(L,\mathcal{G})$] the verdict of the anchor test on log $L$;
\item[$\epsilon$] the depth-matching tolerance, equal to $10^{-6}$\,mm, i.e.\ exact agreement up to floating-point noise;
\item[$\vee$] logical or, meaning that either discrepancy alone suffices to conclude forgery.
\end{symbolist}
Naming the two discrepancies separately matters: time-shifting alters
$\Delta_{\mathrm{time}}$ only, depth inflation alters
$\Delta_{\mathrm{depth}}$ only, and omission and addition alter both, so a
single comparison catches every fraud class.

\begin{proposition}[Completeness of the anchor]
\label{prop:complete}
If a claimed log $L$ differs from the gateway record $\mathcal{G}$ in the
multiset of hours or in any depth at a shared hour, then $A(L,\mathcal{G})$
returns \text{forge}. If $L$ and $\mathcal{G}$ agree on both, then $L$ is a
faithful transcript and no forgery of the three quantities in \eqref{eq:three}
has occurred.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Supplementary~S12: the first clause is the definition of \eqref{eq:anchor},
and the second follows because all three forgeable quantities are functions of
the hour set and the per-hour depths.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Why this does not re-introduce false accusation}

Proposition~\ref{prop:complete} is strong enough to look dangerous: any
discrepancy is treated as forgery, including the forgetting and rounding that
Sec.~\ref{sec:guarantee} showed to be ubiquitous. The resolution is that the
anchor compares a transcript against the \emph{source}, not a log against a
\emph{model}: an honest farmer who forgets an entry is simply completed from
$\mathcal{G}$. False accusation then requires the gateway to disagree with
itself --- a device-integrity question --- which is why Table~\ref{tab:falseacc}
holds at $0/60$ while the behavioural tiers hold at $26.7\%$ and $5.0\%$
(Supplementary~S18).

\subsection{Cost and the scope of the claim}

The anchor requires the gateway of the companion work~\cite{twingate2026},
which actuates the pump and already writes a signed, time-stamped command
record as part of its safety function; the marginal cost of attestation is
storage and transmission of a record that exists regardless (Supplementary Table~S4). The scope is
nonetheless narrower than the premise we started from: a hand-written log, with
no device in the loop, is \emph{not} admissible evidence on its own, since
Sec.~\ref{sec:results-attack} measures a rational adversary inflating credits by
$+35\%$ while escaping every physics- and volume-based test ($6/6$ altered
logs). Near-zero-\emph{marginal}-cost MRV is achievable only where a controller stands
between
the farmer and the pump; where irrigation is manual this layer does not
apply.
