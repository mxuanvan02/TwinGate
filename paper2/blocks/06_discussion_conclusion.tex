\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Relation to prior work}

AWD monitoring has been approached from three directions --- instrumented
water-level sensors~\cite{delacruz2022}, remote
sensing~\cite{lovell2019,islam2026alose,mlrice2025}, and programme-level
MRV~\cite{qd1490,mae2026progress,irri2026mrv} --- while agronomic
meta-analysis~\cite{carrijo2017} and adoption
studies~\cite{lampayan2015,enriquez2021} explain why instrumented monitoring
has not spread. None evaluates the \emph{reliability of the action record
itself}, which is the question asked here (full survey in Supplementary~S8). Satellite radar, the obvious low-cost alternative, we
measured rather than argued about: Table~\ref{tab:benchmark} scores every
route by how many of the $150$ credit-eligible dry phases it could confirm, a
phase counting only if at least one observation falls inside
it~\cite{clauss2017s1,hashemi2022s1,delacruz2022}. Two cautions: the column
measures the ability to \emph{earn} a credit, not to detect fraud --- tube and
command record tie at $100\%$ but differ in cost and in what they prove ---
and photograph-plus-self-report scores $0\%$ because it produces no
observation series to reconcile against. Radar therefore keeps the
role VM0051 Appendix 4 assigns it: a per-plot cross-check lowering the
frequency of field verification~\cite{vm0051} (Supplementary~S9.2).

\subsection{Limitations}

Seven limitations bound the claims, all stated in full in Supplementary~S21;
the two that condition the headline results are these. \emph{Simulated
farmers}: forgeries are injected into simulated trajectories, so a field pilot
is still needed to measure behavioural response --- above all whether farmers
shift their \emph{actual} pumping once the anchor is known, an attack that
changes $M$ rather than $L$ and that our model cannot represent. \emph{The
attack is demonstrated in the water-scarce year only}: in 2025 most logs fall
below the three-command minimum at which a timing attack is constructible ---
of $37$ genuinely altered logs under the full $\Theta$, all $37$ come from
2024. That escarpment is itself a finding (MRV fraud risk is concentrated
where water is scarce), but the attack results are not yet evidence about a
wet season; under the reduced $\Theta$ twelve altered logs do come from 2025
and behave the same way --- suggestive rather than conclusive. The remaining
five are enumerated in S21.

\section{Conclusion}

We set out to make rice MRV nearly free by auditing a farmer-written irrigation
log against soil-water physics. The first version of that layer reported no
false accusation and complete detection; both numbers were artefacts of a field
generated by the auditing model itself, and rebuilding it against an adversary
that optimises the credited quantity changes the conclusion.

The point-model detector falsely accuses $26.7\%$ of honest farmers; a
proportional threshold and an intersection over a $150$-element uncertainty
set reduce that to $5.0\%$ and $0/60$ at a measured cost in detection ($9/9$
to $4/9$ on omission fraud). Meanwhile a volume-preserving permutation of
claimed times inflates creditable dry phases by $33\%$ to $38\%$ while
escaping the robust tier in $8$ of $9$ and $3$ of $3$ altered logs, and no
physical configuration escapes that trade-off: delaying a claim both lengthens
the dry phase and increases the deficit available to it, so profit and
undetectability share a gradient.

The repair is not hydrological. Matching the claimed log against the gateway's
signed, time-stamped command record catches $116/116$ genuinely forged logs and
accuses none of $60$ honest ones, at no cost beyond a controller the companion
work already deploys. What survives of the original premise: physics
\emph{constrains} a log, but only a signed device record \emph{attests} it
--- near-zero-cost MRV is achievable only where a device stands between the
farmer and the pump. Three previously published numbers are corrected
accordingly ($[49.0,86.0]$\,mm rather than $61$\,mm; an inert third filter;
an inadmissible absolute gap test). Code, both uncertainty sets, all $281$
audited logs and $46$ unit tests are public.
