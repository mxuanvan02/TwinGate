\section{Threat Model}
\label{sec:threat}

A claimed irrigation log is a finite map
\begin{equation}
L=\{(t_i,u_i)\}_{i=1}^{|L|},\qquad u_i>0,\ t_i\in[0,T),
\label{eq:log}
\end{equation}
where:
\begin{symbolist}
\item[$L$] the claimed log for one season, listing the irrigation events the farmer
  recorded;
\item[$|L|$] the number of irrigation events in the log;
\item[$u_i$] the claimed irrigation depth of event $i$, that is, the water the farmer
  records as applied to the field, in millimetres. This is the thickness of the water
  layer if that volume were spread evenly over the field surface, not a groundwater
  depth and not a soil-layer depth; it carries the same unit as the field capacity
  $FC$ and the root-zone storage $w_t$ defined in Sec.~\ref{sec:model};
\item[$t_i$] the hour index of event $i$, taking values from 0 to $T$ as defined in
  Sec.~\ref{sec:model}.
\end{symbolist} Three
independent quantities can be forged, and each defence tier observes a different
subset of them:
\begin{equation}
\underbrace{\textstyle\sum_i u_i}_{\text{total volume}}\,,\qquad
\underbrace{\{u_i\}}_{\text{individual irrigation depths}}\,,\qquad
\underbrace{\{t_i\}}_{\text{timestamps}}\,.
\label{eq:three}
\end{equation}
Supplementary Table~S1 lists the fraud classes corresponding to each. The adversary is
\emph{rational}: it maximises creditable dry phases subject to not being
flagged, and it knows the detector, because the detector is fully specified in this paper
and its source code is public; this is the sense in which $\varepsilon$ in \eqref{eq:v4} below is not a secret: it
is a published tolerance for model error, set to $5$\,mm throughout the experiments
and equal to the value in the public source code. It is distinct from the pump-gap
threshold $\tau_0$ of Sec.~\ref{sec:guarantee}.

The first defence tier compares the total claimed volume with the volume actually
pumped. Let $M$ be the volume actually pumped, measured at the cooperative station,
and $U(L)=\sum_i u_i$ the claimed total. The tier-1 statistic is the gap
\begin{equation}
G=M-U(L),
\label{eq:gap}
\end{equation}
where:
\begin{symbolist}
\item[$G$] the pump gap, in mm, being the volume really pumped in excess of the
  claimed total;
\item[$M$] the total volume actually pumped over the season, measured at the
  cooperative station, in mm;
\item[$U(L)$] the sum of the depths claimed in log $L$, in mm.
\end{symbolist}

A positive $G$ means the farmer under-reported. The credit-relevant quantity, however,
is not $G$ but the number of \emph{dry phases}. Write $\mathcal{A}(\cdot)$ for the set of maximal intervals
$[a,b)$ of a replayed
trajectory over which the depletion stays at or above $d_{\mathrm{dry}}$ with
no claimed irrigation, lasting at least $72$\,h --- the three-day minimum of
Decision 4801~\cite{qd4801}:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{A}\bigl(\{\hat d_t\}\bigr)=\Bigl\{[a,b):\ &\min_{t\in[a,b)}
\hat d_t\ge d_{\mathrm{dry}},\\
&u_t=0\ \forall t\in[a,b),\\
&b-a\ge 72\,\mathrm{h}\Bigr\}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:awd}
\end{equation}
where:
\begin{symbolist}
\item[$\mathcal{A}(\cdot)$] the operator returning the set of credit-eligible dry
  phases of a depletion trajectory;
\item[$[a,b)$] a contiguous interval of hours, starting at hour $a$ and ending before
  hour $b$, maximal in the sense that it cannot be extended further;
\item[$\hat d_t$] depletion at hour $t$ implied by the log, defined in
  Sec.~\ref{sec:model};
\item[$d_{\mathrm{dry}}$] the minimum depletion for an interval to count as a dry
  phase, a dimensionless fraction;
\item[$u_t$] the irrigation depth applied at hour $t$, equal to $0$ throughout a dry
  interval;
\item[$b-a$] the length of the interval, in hours.
\end{symbolist}

The credited count is then
\begin{equation}
n_{\mathrm{dry}}(L)=\bigl|\mathcal{A}\bigl(\mathcal{R}(L)\bigr)\bigr|,
\label{eq:ndry}
\end{equation}
where $n_{\mathrm{dry}}(L)$ is the number of credit-eligible dry phases of log $L$,
$\mathcal{R}(L)$ is the replayed trajectory of that log under \eqref{eq:replay}, and
$|\cdot|$ counts the elements of a set. The threshold $d_{\mathrm{dry}}=0.45$ is used
throughout; this value is a modelling choice of ours,
set high enough that a dry phase is meaningful rather than merely not flooded (its
sensitivity is examined in Appendix~S20). Revenue is proportional to
$n_{\mathrm{dry}}$, not to $U(L)$ --- the distinction that makes the attack of
Sec.~\ref{sec:timeshift} profitable, invisible in a formulation that treats
total volume as the target.

\section{Physics-Based Filters and Their Exact Blind Spots}
\label{sec:filters}

Replaying $L$ under public ERA5 forcing produces $(\hat w_t,\hat d_t)$ (the
companion work~\cite{twingate2026} gives the balance in full); three filters
apply. \emph{$V_1$, saturation}: a claim issued while the root zone is already
at field capacity cannot be stored:
\begin{equation}
V_1(L)=\{\,i:\ u_i>0\ \wedge\ \hat w_{t_i}^{-}\ \ge\ FC-\varepsilon\,\}.
\label{eq:v1}
\end{equation}

where $V_1(L)$ is the set of indices of the claims flagged by the first filter,
$\hat w_{t_i}^{-}$ is the root-zone water content immediately before event $i$ is
applied, defined in Sec.~\ref{sec:model}, and $\varepsilon=5$~mm is the published
tolerance for model error, shared by $V_1$ and $V_4$.

\emph{$V_4$, storage excess}: a claim larger than the deficit available at
that hour must have run off or percolated, so it was not ``applied'' in the
sense the standard requires:
\begin{equation}
V_4(L)=\{\,i:\ u_i\ >\ \underbrace{(FC-\hat w_{t_i}^{-})}_{\text{deficit}}
\ +\ \varepsilon\,\}.
\label{eq:v4}
\end{equation}

where $V_4(L)$ is the set of indices of the flagged claims, and the underbraced
expression is the deficit, that is, the water the root zone can still accept at that
hour before reaching field capacity.

Both $V_1$ and $V_4$ test the \emph{claimed} depth $u_i$, the same quantity the pump
meter records, rather than $\eta u_i$. This is deliberate: when a log is checked
against metered pumping both sides must carry the same unit, and folding $\eta$ into
the claimed side would blur the threshold by the very quantity that is uncertain.
The efficiency enters only the replay equation \eqref{eq:bucket}, where it shapes
the trajectory $(\hat w_t,\hat d_t)$ and not the reconciliation of records.

\emph{$V_5$, infiltration bound.} Independent of state, a single event of
duration $\tau_i$ cannot deliver more than the soil can accept:
\begin{equation}
u_i\ \le\ k_{\mathrm{inf}}\,\tau_i+p_{\max},
\label{eq:v5}
\end{equation}
with $k_{\mathrm{inf}}=10$\,mm\,h$^{-1}$ and $p_{\max}=100$\,mm at the
loose end of the published range for puddled rice~\cite{kukal2004,bouman2001}
(bound $110$\,mm at $\tau_i=1$\,h), so that a farmer who records two adjacent
events in one hour is not accused.


where:
\begin{symbolist}
\item[$\tau_i$] the duration of irrigation event $i$, in hours;
\item[$k_{\mathrm{inf}}$] the maximum infiltration rate of the field, in mm per hour;
\item[$p_{\max}$] the maximum water the field accepts in one event regardless of
    duration, in mm.
\end{symbolist}

A fourth filter, on depletion below the wilting point, was included in this layer. It cannot activate on dry-season delta rice: see Remark~\ref{sec:v2reach}.

\begin{proposition}[Exact blindness of aggregate defences]
\label{prop:blind}
Let $L$ and $L'$ share the same multiset of depth-and-duration pairs and the same
total, i.e.\ $\{(u_i,\tau_i)\}=\{(u'_j,\tau'_j)\}$ as multisets, with each
duration carried along by its event when that event is moved. Then
\begin{equation}
G(L)=G(L')\qquad\text{and}\qquad |V_5(L)|=|V_5(L')|.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Supplementary~S11; both quantities are symmetric functions of the depth
multiset and never depend on $t_i$.
\end{proof}

The scope of the result should be stated plainly. Proposition~\ref{prop:blind}
proves invariance of exactly two quantities, $G$ and $|V_5|$, and therefore establishes
only that these two aggregate defences are blind to time shifting. It is not an
impossibility theorem for every detector built on a replayed trajectory: a detector
designed to compare the timing of each command directly against an independent time
source lies outside its scope, and the device anchor of Sec.~\ref{sec:anchor} is
precisely such a detector. For the remaining filters what this paper reports is a
\emph{measured} trade-off between evasion and false accusation
(Table~\ref{tab:defence}), not a proof that they can never fire.

The two defences that do not depend on the model being right --- the pump-meter
reconciliation $G$ of \eqref{eq:gap} and the infiltration bound $V_5$ --- are
\emph{exactly} invariant under the transformation the adversary needs. What remains is
$V_1$ and $V_4$, which do depend on order through $\hat w_{t_i}^{-}$; in our
experiments both fire rarely, but that is a measurement rather than a necessity.

\section{The Time-Shifting Attack}
\label{sec:timeshift}

\begin{proposition}[Credit gain and evasion are the same action]
\label{prop:coincide}
Let a claim at hour $t$ have available deficit $c_t=FC-\hat w_t^{-}$, and let the
waiting interval to the next claim be a \emph{net-loss interval}, that is,
$\Delta_t=\sum_{s}(\mathit{ETc}_s+\mathit{DP}_s-P_s^{+})\ge 0$ with $P_s^{+}$ the
rainfall net of runoff. This condition does not always hold, since a sufficiently
large rain event can offset evapotranspiration and reduce the deficit. It is stated
as a hypothesis because the proof needs nothing beyond it, and because in practice
the adversary reads the same public forcing series as the auditor and can select
exactly those intervals that satisfy it; how often they do is measured in
Supplement~S11. Delaying a claim by $\delta$ hours then increases its deficit by the
drainage accrued in those hours:
\begin{equation}
c_{t+\delta}\ \ge\ c_t+\min\!\bigl(\delta\cdot\overline{\Delta},\ FC-\hat w_t^{-}\bigr).
\label{eq:delay}
\end{equation}
where:
\begin{symbolist}
\item[$c_t$] the deficit at hour $t$, in mm, equal to the water the root zone can
  still accept before reaching field capacity;
\item[$\Delta_t$] the water the bucket loses between two consecutive claims, in mm;
  the hypothesis of the proposition is that this quantity is nonnegative, and that
  hypothesis can fail over an interval with effective rainfall;
\item[$\mathit{ETc}_s$] crop evapotranspiration at hour $s$, that is, $ET_{c,s}$ of
  \eqref{eq:etc};
\item[$\mathit{DP}_s$] deep percolation below the root zone at hour $s$, the water
  removed by the clipping operator in \eqref{eq:bucket};
\item[$P_s^{+}$] the rainfall actually entering the field at hour $s$, after surface
  runoff $RO_s$ has been subtracted;
\item[$\delta$] the number of hours by which a claim is delayed;
\item[$\overline{\Delta}$] the mean water loss per hour over the waiting interval.
\end{symbolist}

All three terms of $\Delta_t$ come from the same bucket equation \eqref{eq:bucket}, so
this inequality rests on no new hydrological assumption. When a command is recorded
later than it was issued, the field dries further during the wait, so the number of dry
phases rises, while the drier soil absorbs exactly that water and no physical filter
sees anything anomalous.

Consequently the transformation that moves claims \emph{out of} a dry interval
and onto its boundary simultaneously (i)~increases $n_{\mathrm{dry}}$, because
the interval is no longer interrupted, and (ii)~increases $c_t$ at every moved
claim, which by \eqref{eq:v4} makes $V_4$ \emph{less} likely to fire. There is
no trade-off for the adversary to negotiate: the gradient of profit and the
gradient of undetectability point the same way.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Supplementary~S11. In short: (i) is definitional, since $\mathcal{A}$ requires
no claim inside the interval; (ii) follows because delaying a claim lets the
bucket drain for $\delta$ further hours with no inflow, so the available
deficit can only grow while $u_i$ is unchanged.
\end{proof}

This is why the attack cannot be closed by better hydrology: among the filters this paper evaluates, those whose verdict is a function of the
replayed trajectory must integrate the same drainage that makes the delayed claim
look legitimate, and the two aggregate defences are invariant outright by
Proposition~\ref{prop:blind}. A detector that compares the timing of each command
against an independent time source is not subject to this constraint, and that is
exactly the device anchor of Sec.~\ref{sec:anchor}. The empirical attack is
a hill-climb over integer hour offsets in $[-36,36]$ accepting a move only if
the total is preserved bit-for-bit, every depth stays under \eqref{eq:v5}, and
$n_{\mathrm{dry}}$ strictly increases. The attack does not steal water ---
what is stolen is \emph{history} (Supplementary~S19).
