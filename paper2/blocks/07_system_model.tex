\section{System Model}
\label{sec:model}

\subsection{Field water balance}

The root-zone water content $w_t$ [mm] evolves hourly under the FAO-56 bucket
model~\cite{fao56}:
\begin{equation}
w_{t+1}=\mathrm{clip}_{\le FC}\!\left(w_t+P_t-RO_t-ET_{c,t}+u_t\right),
\label{eq:bucket}
\end{equation}
with crop evapotranspiration and a water-stress coefficient
\begin{equation}
ET_{c,t}=K_{s,t}\,K_c\,ET_{0,t},
\qquad
K_{s,t}=\frac{FC-w_t}{(1-p)\,\mathrm{TAW}},
\label{eq:etc}
\end{equation}
where $ET_{0,t}$ is hourly reference evapotranspiration (Penman-Monteith),
$P_t$ rainfall, $RO_t$ surface runoff (Soil Conservation Service [SCS] curve-number, evaluated daily and
shared pro rata over the hours of a rain event), and $u_t$ the irrigation depth
applied in hour $t$. Drainage above field capacity $FC$ is removed by the
clipping operator, so mass conservation holds exactly and is checked as an
invariant. The root-zone depletion fraction is
\begin{equation}
d_t=\frac{FC-w_t}{FC-WP},\qquad \mathrm{span}=FC-WP,
\label{eq:depl}
\end{equation}
with wilting point $WP$; for the reference silty clay loam of the delta,
$\mathrm{TAW}=FC-WP=70$\,mm, $FC=160$\,mm, $WP=90$\,mm.

\subsection{Replay of a claimed log}
\label{sec:replay}

Let a claimed log supply the irrigation term of \eqref{eq:bucket}. The
\emph{replay operator} $\mathcal{R}$ maps a log to the trajectory it implies, by
iterating \eqref{eq:bucket} under the observed forcing $(P_t,ET_{0,t})$:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{R}(L)=\{(\hat w_t,\hat d_t)\}_{t=0}^{T},
\label{eq:replay}
\end{equation}
with $\hat w_0=w_{\mathrm{init}}$ and
\begin{equation}
u_t=\sum_{(t_i,u_i)\in L}u_i\,\mathbb{1}[t_i=t],
\label{eq:uterm}
\end{equation}
so that $\hat w_t$ is the root-zone water content the log \emph{claims} the
field had, and $\hat d_t$ its depletion fraction under \eqref{eq:depl}; the
hat marks quantities reconstructed from the log rather than measured. Replay is
deterministic and uses only public reanalysis, so two auditors replaying the
same log obtain the same trajectory and the verdict is reproducible. A
superscript minus denotes the state immediately \emph{before} that hour's
claim is applied, as in $\hat w_{t}^{-}$: the water available to receive an
event, which is what a filter must inspect.

The operator is also what makes the scheme cheap: no sensor is queried, and the
only inputs are the log, the public weather series and a soil profile. Its
weakness, developed in Sec.~\ref{sec:timeshift}, is that it can only check
whether a trajectory is \emph{possible}, never whether it \emph{happened}.
