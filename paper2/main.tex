\begin{abstract}
Measurement, reporting and verification (MRV) is the bottleneck keeping emission
reductions in low-emission rice from becoming tradable credits, and monitoring is
about three quarters of that cost. Because every credit-eligible water-management
action is already an \emph{actuation command}, a farmer-written irrigation log
looks like free evidence of the highest rank under Decision 4801/QD-BNNMT. We
built such a layer, proved it cannot accuse a compliant farmer, and then attacked
it. The attack is the contribution. Replaying a log through the FAO-56 water
balance under public ERA5 forcing exposes claimed depths no soil can hold, but
an adversary who keeps the total volume and every individual depth intact and
merely \emph{shifts commands in time} inflates creditable dry phases by
$69\%$ and escapes both the physics layer and the pump meter in $91\%$ of cases.
We measure this over $281$ audited logs at three Mekong Delta stations across two
climate years, with the field deliberately violating every modelling assumption:
soil texture across the FAO-56 Table 2 range, staged crop coefficient,
irrigation efficiency $0.60$--$0.85$, field rainfall differing from the grid
cell, bund seepage, and farmers who forget, round and record late. Under those
conditions the published point-model detector falsely accused $26.7\%$ of honest
farmers. Matching the log against the gateway's signed, time-stamped command
record instead catches $116/116$ genuinely forged logs and accuses none. The
result is a negative one with a constructive repair: physics-based attestation is
worth having but cannot stand alone, and the timestamp must come from a device
rather than from the farmer.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Viet Nam has committed to one million hectares of low-emission rice by 2030, and
the agronomy is settled: raising the severity of intermittent drying cut methane
emission by $41$--$71\%$ against continuous flooding in irrigated delta rice
with no grain-yield penalty~\cite{balaine2019}, and water regime is among the
dominant controls on seasonal methane flux across Asian rice
fields~\cite{yan2005}. What is not settled is measurement. Under
Decision 4801/QD-BNNMT the highest-ranked evidence for a credit claim is a record
of the irrigation and drainage actions actually taken~\cite{qd4801}, and under
Verra's VM0051 the same record must survive third-party
verification~\cite{vm0051}. Producing it costs money that a smallholder
cooperative does not have: monitoring alone accounts for about $77\%$ of MRV
expenditure in comparable programmes~\cite{jem2026mrv}, and the current procedure
relies on periodic photographs of a water-level tube.

The obvious repair is to notice that a modern irrigation command is already a
digital event. If a gateway actuates the pump, it can also record the actuation,
and that record is a Tier-1 activity datum in the sense of Decision 4801 at no
marginal cost. This is the premise of the companion work~\cite{twingate2026},
which uses an FAO-56 twin as a safety gate deciding whether a command may be
transmitted at all over a lossy channel.

We took the next step and asked whether the same twin could \emph{audit} what a
farmer claims after the fact, with no sensor deployed: a log of claimed depths is
replayed through the water balance under public ERA5 forcing, and a claim that the
soil could not have absorbed is evidence of fabrication. A first version of this
layer reported no false accusation on $60$ honest logs and detection of $60/60$
forged ones. That result was correct and worthless, because the trajectories
labelled ``real'' were generated by the same model that then audited them, with
the same default soil profile, the same noiseless forcing, irrigation as a
one-hour impulse of exactly $\{20,40\}$\,mm, and a farmer who records the right
depth at the right hour every time. The detector was grading its own homework.

This paper reports what happens when that circularity is removed. We rebuild the
field so that it violates every assumption of the twin, and we replace the naive
fraud patterns with an adversary that has read the detector. Three findings
follow.

First, the point-model detector is not merely optimistic, it is \emph{unsafe}:
under realistic soil texture, staged crop coefficient, irrigation efficiency
below unity, and recording noise, it falsely accuses $26.7\%$ of honest farmers,
because an absolute pump-gap threshold treats a farmer who forgets one entry or
rounds to the nearest $5$\,mm exactly like one who irrigated secretly.

Second, and more damaging to the premise, physics-based attestation has a hole
that no amount of modelling closes. An adversary who preserves the total pumped
volume and keeps every individual claimed depth below the infiltration bound can
simply \emph{shift commands in time}. Total volume unchanged leaves the pump
meter blind; individual depths unchanged leave the soil-capacity filter blind;
and because the water balance integrates, the resulting trajectory is perfectly
self-consistent. What changes is the number of dry phases of at least $72$
hours --- precisely the quantity that VM0051 and Decision 4801 pay for. We
measure an inflation of $69\%$ under the reduced uncertainty set and $78\%$
under the full one, escaping both defence tiers in $91\%$ of cases.

Third, the repair is cheap and it is not physical. Because the gateway that
actuates the pump also holds a signed, time-stamped command record, the claimed
log can be matched against what the device actually did. This catches $116/116$
genuinely forged logs while accusing none of $60$ honest ones. It is the only
defence in our evaluation that reaches both figures, and it costs nothing beyond
the gateway that the companion work already deploys.

The contributions are as follows.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{An adversarial evaluation of physics-attested MRV.} We formalise a
threat model in which the adversary may alter the total volume, the individual
depths and the timestamps of a claimed irrigation log, and we identify which of
the three each defence tier can observe
(Sec.~\ref{sec:threat}, Table~\ref{tab:threat}).
\item \emph{A time-shifting attack that defeats soil-water physics.} We prove
that any detector depending only on the replayed water balance is blind to a
volume-preserving, depth-bounded permutation of claimed times, and we measure the
resulting credit inflation at $69$--$78\%$
(Sec.~\ref{sec:timeshift}, Table~\ref{tab:credit}).
\item \emph{A device-anchored defence with a measured guarantee.} Matching the
claimed log against the gateway command record catches every genuinely forged log
in the evaluation and produces no false accusation
(Sec.~\ref{sec:anchor}, Table~\ref{tab:defence}).
\item \emph{A robust accusation rule under model uncertainty.} Replacing the
point model by an intersection over an uncertainty set
$\Theta$ of $30$ soil, crop-coefficient and efficiency configurations restores the
no-false-accusation property when the true field is unknown, at a measured cost
in detection power that we report rather than hide
(Sec.~\ref{sec:robust}, Table~\ref{tab:falseacc}).
\item \emph{Three corrections to previously published numbers.} The admissible
depth bound is a range $[49.0,86.0]$\,mm across $\Theta$ rather than a single
$61$\,mm; the third physical filter cannot activate on dry-season delta rice
because clipped depletion peaks at $0.9987$; and a proportional pump-gap
threshold is required, the absolute one having a $26.7\%$ false-accusation rate
(Sec.~\ref{sec:umax}, Sec.~\ref{sec:v2reach}, Table~\ref{tab:umax},
Table~\ref{tab:f2reach}).
\end{enumerate}

The remainder of the paper gives the system model and the three filters
(Sec.~\ref{sec:model}, Sec.~\ref{sec:filters}), the guarantees and where they
break (Sec.~\ref{sec:guarantee}), the attack and the defence
(Sec.~\ref{sec:timeshift}, Sec.~\ref{sec:anchor}), the cost model
(Sec.~\ref{sec:cost}), and the evaluation (Sec.~\ref{sec:design},
Sec.~\ref{sec:results}).

\section{System Model}
\label{sec:model}

\subsection{Field water balance}

The root-zone water content $w_t$ [mm] evolves hourly under the FAO-56 bucket
model~\cite{fao56}:
\begin{equation}
w_{t+1}=\mathrm{clip}_{\le FC}\!\left(w_t+P_t-RO_t-ET_{c,t}+u_t\right),
\label{eq:bucket}
\end{equation}
with crop evapotranspiration and a water-stress coefficient
\begin{equation}
ET_{c,t}=K_{s,t}\,K_c\,ET_{0,t},
\qquad
K_{s,t}=\frac{FC-w_t}{(1-p)\,\mathrm{TAW}},
\label{eq:etc}
\end{equation}
where $ET_{0,t}$ is the hourly reference evapotranspiration from the
Penman-Monteith equation, $P_t$ is rainfall, $RO_t$ is surface runoff from the
SCS curve-number method evaluated at daily resolution and shared pro rata over
the hours of a rain event, and $u_t$ is the irrigation depth applied in hour
$t$. Drainage above field capacity $FC$ is removed by the clipping operator, so
mass conservation holds exactly and is checked as an invariant. The root-zone
depletion fraction is
\begin{equation}
d_t=\frac{FC-w_t}{FC-WP},\qquad \mathrm{span}=FC-WP,
\label{eq:depl}
\end{equation}
with wilting point $WP$. For the reference silty clay loam of the delta,
$\mathrm{TAW}=70$\,mm, $FC=160$\,mm, $WP=90$\,mm, so $\mathrm{span}=70$\,mm.

\section{Threat Model}
\label{sec:threat}

A claimed irrigation log is a finite map
\begin{equation}
L=\{(t_i,u_i)\}_{i=1}^{|L|},\qquad u_i>0,\ t_i\in[0,T),
\label{eq:log}
\end{equation}
where $u_i$ is a claimed depth in millimetres and $t_i$ an hour index. Three
independent quantities can be forged, and each defence tier observes a different
subset of them:
\begin{equation}
\underbrace{\textstyle\sum_i u_i}_{\text{total volume}}\,,\qquad
\underbrace{\{u_i\}}_{\text{individual depths}}\,,\qquad
\underbrace{\{t_i\}}_{\text{timestamps}}\,.
\label{eq:three}
\end{equation}
Table~\ref{tab:threat} lists the resulting fraud classes. The adversary is
\emph{rational}: it maximises creditable dry phases subject to not being flagged,
and it knows the detector, because the detector is published. Publishing a
threshold is publishing the constraint the adversary optimises against; this is
the sense in which $\varepsilon$ in \eqref{eq:v4} below is not a secret.

Let $M$ be the volume actually pumped, measured at the cooperative station, and
$U(L)=\sum_i u_i$ the claimed total. The tier-1 statistic is
\begin{equation}
G=M-U(L),
\label{eq:gap}
\end{equation}
and the credit-relevant quantity is the number of maximal intervals on which the
replayed depletion stays above $d_{\mathrm{dry}}$ with no claim, of length at
least $72$\,h:
\begin{equation}
n_{\mathrm{dry}}(L)=\bigl|\mathcal{A}\bigl(\mathcal{R}(L)\bigr)\bigr|.
\label{eq:ndry}
\end{equation}
Revenue is proportional to $n_{\mathrm{dry}}$, not to $U(L)$. This distinction is
what makes the attack of Sec.~\ref{sec:timeshift} profitable, and it is invisible
in a formulation that treats total volume as the target.

\section{Physics-Based Filters and Their Exact Blind Spots}
\label{sec:filters}

Replaying $L$ through the FAO-56 root-zone bucket~\cite{fao56} under public ERA5
forcing produces a trajectory $(\hat w_t,\hat d_t)$; the companion
work~\cite{twingate2026} gives the balance in full. Three filters apply.

\emph{$V_1$, saturation.} A claim issued while the root zone is already at field
capacity cannot be stored:
\begin{equation}
V_1(L)=\{\,i:\ u_i>0\ \wedge\ \hat w_{t_i}^{-}\ \ge\ FC-\varepsilon\,\}.
\label{eq:v1}
\end{equation}

\emph{$V_4$, storage excess.} A claim larger than the deficit available at that
hour must have run off or percolated, so it was not ``applied'' in the sense the
standard requires:
\begin{equation}
V_4(L)=\{\,i:\ u_i\ >\ \underbrace{(FC-\hat w_{t_i}^{-})}_{\text{deficit}}
\ +\ \varepsilon\,\}.
\label{eq:v4}
\end{equation}

\emph{$V_5$, infiltration bound.} Independent of state, a single event of
duration $\tau_i$ cannot deliver more than the soil can accept:
\begin{equation}
u_i\ \le\ k_{\mathrm{inf}}\,\tau_i+p_{\max},
\label{eq:v5}
\end{equation}
with $k_{\mathrm{inf}}=10$\,mm\,h$^{-1}$ and $p_{\max}=100$\,mm taken at the
loose end of the published range for puddled rice, so that a farmer who records
two adjacent events in one hour is not accused. At $\tau_i=1$\,h the bound is
$110$\,mm.

A fourth filter, on depletion below the wilting point, was included in the
published version of this layer. It cannot activate: see
Sec.~\ref{sec:v2reach}.

\begin{proposition}[Exact blindness of aggregate defences]
\label{prop:blind}
Let $L$ and $L'$ share the same multiset of depths and the same total, i.e.\
$\{u_i\}=\{u'_j\}$ as multisets. Then
\begin{equation}
G(L)=G(L')\qquad\text{and}\qquad |V_5(L)|=|V_5(L')|.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$U(L)=\sum_i u_i$ is a symmetric function of the multiset, so it is invariant
under any reassignment of depths to hours; $M$ is a property of the field, not of
the log; hence $G=M-U$ is unchanged. Likewise \eqref{eq:v5} is evaluated per
event and depends only on $(u_i,\tau_i)$, never on $t_i$ or on $\hat w$, so the
count of violations is a function of the multiset alone.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:blind} is the whole difficulty in one line. The pump meter
and the infiltration bound are the two defences that do not depend on the model
being right, and both are \emph{exactly} invariant under the transformation the
adversary needs. What remains is $V_1$ and $V_4$, which do depend on order
through $\hat w_{t_i}^{-}$. They turn out not to help.

\section{The Time-Shifting Attack}
\label{sec:timeshift}

\begin{proposition}[Credit gain and evasion are the same action]
\label{prop:coincide}
Let a claim at hour $t$ have available deficit $c_t=FC-\hat w_t^{-}$. Between
consecutive claims the bucket loses
$\Delta_t=\sum_{s}(\mathit{ETc}_s+\mathit{DP}_s-P_s^{+})\ge 0$, so delaying a
claim by $\delta$ hours increases its deficit by the drainage accrued in those
hours:
\begin{equation}
c_{t+\delta}\ \ge\ c_t+\min\!\bigl(\delta\cdot\overline{\Delta},\ FC-\hat w_t^{-}\bigr).
\label{eq:delay}
\end{equation}
Consequently the transformation that moves claims \emph{out of} a dry interval
and onto its boundary simultaneously
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] increases $n_{\mathrm{dry}}$, because the interval is no longer
interrupted; and
\item[(ii)] increases $c_t$ at every moved claim, which by \eqref{eq:v4} makes
$V_4$ \emph{less} likely to fire.
\end{enumerate}
There is no trade-off for the adversary to negotiate: the gradient of profit and
the gradient of undetectability point the same way.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) is definitional: $\mathcal{A}$ in \eqref{eq:ndry} requires no claim inside
the interval, so removing one extends or creates a qualifying phase. For (ii),
delaying a claim to $t+\delta$ means the bucket continues to lose water for
$\delta$ further hours with no inflow, so $\hat w_{t+\delta}^{-}\le\hat w_t^{-}$
and $c_{t+\delta}\ge c_t$; the bound \eqref{eq:delay} follows because the loss
per hour is at least the mean $\overline{\Delta}$ until field capacity is
reached, after which $c$ saturates at $FC-\hat w$. Since $V_4$ fires only when
$u_i>c_{t_i}+\varepsilon$ and $u_i$ is unchanged, a larger $c_{t_i}$ can only
reduce the violation set.
\end{proof}

This is why the attack cannot be closed by better hydrology. Any detector whose
verdict is a function of the replayed trajectory must integrate the same
drainage that makes the delayed claim look legitimate. The empirical version of
the attack is a hill-climb over integer hour offsets in $[-36,36]$ that accepts a
move only if the total is preserved bit-for-bit, every depth stays under
\eqref{eq:v5}, and $n_{\mathrm{dry}}$ strictly increases.

\begin{remark}
The attack does not steal water. Total pumped volume is unchanged, so the farmer
pays the same and the atmosphere sees the same methane. What is stolen is
\emph{history}: the credit is issued for a dry-phase pattern that did not occur.
Under VM0051 the crediting unit is precisely that pattern, so the loss is real
even though no physical resource moved.
\end{remark}

\section{When May a Farmer Be Accused?}
\label{sec:guarantee}

\subsection{The admissible depth is a range, not a number}
\label{sec:umax}

The published version of this layer stated a single bound, $u\le 61$\,mm, as the
largest claim that never raises an alarm. That number is an artefact of one soil
profile and one initial condition. With $V_4$ firing when $u_i>c_{t_i}+\varepsilon$
and $c_t=d_t\cdot\mathrm{span}$ where $\mathrm{span}=FC-WP$, the bound at a field
whose initial depletion is $w_0$ and whose operator irrigates no later than
$d^{*}$ is
\begin{equation}
u_{\max}=\max(d^{*},w_0)\cdot\mathrm{span}+\varepsilon .
\label{eq:umax}
\end{equation}
Two corrections follow, both of which we verified by scanning the replay operator
rather than by algebra alone. First, \eqref{eq:umax} uses $\max$, not a
difference: an initially dry field ($w_0=0.90$) admits \emph{larger} claims than a
wet one, and the previously published expression $(d^{*}-w_0)\mathrm{span}+\varepsilon$
yields $43.5$\,mm for $\mathrm{span}=70$\,mm and $w_0=0.25$, which corresponds to
no physical state at all, since the operative deficit at that hour is either
$0.25\,\mathrm{span}$ (initially) or $d^{*}\mathrm{span}$ (once the field has
dried). Second, \eqref{eq:umax} is a bound \emph{per event}, not per season: the
bucket drains continuously through deep percolation and evapotranspiration, so a
season-long claim total is not bounded by $u_{\max}$ and an adversary issuing many
sub-threshold claims can inflate the total without bound. Table~\ref{tab:umax}
gives the range that must be published instead, $[49.0,86.0]$\,mm across
$\Theta$.

\subsection{Robust accusation under model uncertainty}
\label{sec:robust}

The auditor does not know the farmer's soil, the growth stage, or the efficiency
of the delivery system. Let $\Theta$ be the uncertainty set and let
$V_\theta(L)$ be the violation set obtained by replaying under configuration
$\theta$. The published detector evaluated a single $\theta_0$ (the default
profile) and treated every violation as guilt. We replace it by an intersection
over an uncertainty set $\Theta$ built from soil textures $\mathcal{S}$,
crop-coefficient regimes $\mathcal{K}$, irrigation efficiencies $\mathcal{E}$
and initial states $\mathcal{W}$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
V_{\mathrm{rob}}(L)&=\bigcap_{\theta\in\Theta}V_\theta(L),\\
|\mathcal{S}|&=5,\quad|\mathcal{K}|=2,\quad|\mathcal{E}|=3,\quad
|\mathcal{W}|=5,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:robust}
\end{equation}
so $|\Theta|=150$ and each log costs $150$ replays. The semantics are
deliberately one-sided:
\begin{equation}
a(L)=
\begin{cases}
\text{accuse}, & V_{\mathrm{rob}}(L)\neq\emptyset,\\
\text{query}, & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:verdict}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[No false accusation within $\Theta$]
\label{prop:noalarm}
If the true field configuration $\theta^{*}$ belongs to $\Theta$ and $L$ is
compliant under $\theta^{*}$, then $V_{\mathrm{rob}}(L)=\emptyset$ and the farmer
is not accused.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$V_{\mathrm{rob}}(L)\subseteq V_{\theta^{*}}(L)=\emptyset$.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:noalarm} is what makes the layer deployable: model error
can reduce detection power but cannot manufacture an accusation. The price is
real and must be stated. Two implementation details matter, and getting either
wrong silently breaks the proposition. A world that produces \emph{no} violation
must contribute the \emph{empty set} to the intersection; skipping non-firing
worlds computes $\bigcap$ over firing worlds only, which is an existential
quantifier wearing the clothes of a universal one. And $\mathcal{W}$ must span the
full range in which a claim can be legitimate: with
$\mathcal{W}=\{0,0.25\}$ the largest admissible depth anywhere in $\Theta$ is
$22.5$\,mm, so every claim above that is ``guilty in all worlds'' and the
intersection becomes vacuous --- it fires on $102$ of $150$ worlds while $48$
innocent worlds exist. Extending $\mathcal{W}$ to $\{0,0.25,0.5,0.75,0.9\}$
restores the intended semantics.

\subsection{A proportional pump-gap threshold}

The absolute rule $G>\tau_0$ with $\tau_0=5$\,mm assumes the log is a faithful
copy of what was pumped. It is not. Honest farmers forget entries, round to the
nearest $5$\,mm, and record a day late. Each contributes to $G$ without any
wrongdoing, so the threshold must scale with the number of recorded events:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\tau(n)&=\delta_{\mathrm{rel}}M+z\sqrt{n}\,\sigma_{\mathrm{event}},\\
\sigma_{\mathrm{event}}^{2}&=p(1-p)\bar u^{2}+\tfrac{r^{2}}{12}
+q(1-q)(s\bar u)^{2},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:tau}
\end{equation}
with $p$ the forgetting rate, $r$ the rounding step, $q$ the depth-error rate,
$s$ its amplitude, $\bar u=30$\,mm a typical event depth, $\delta_{\mathrm{rel}}=2\%$
and $z=3$. The three variance terms are the forget, round and depth-error
contributions; the absolute rule is the degenerate case $n\to 0$,
$\sigma_{\mathrm{event}}\to 0$.

\section{A Filter That Cannot Fire}
\label{sec:v2reach}

The published layer included a third physical filter, flagging depletion below
the wilting point as physically impossible. It was doubly inoperative. Its
threshold was set at $1.02$ while the reported indicator
$\hat d_t=\min(1,\max(0,\cdot))$ is clipped to $[0,1]$, so the condition was
unsatisfiable by construction: $0$ firings in $281$ logs. We repaired it by
evaluating the unclipped depletion, and then measured whether the repaired filter
can activate at all. Across three stations, two climate years, five textures and
five initial states --- $150$ configurations --- with an empty log, i.e.\ the
driest possible forcing, the unclipped depletion peaks at $0.9987$
(Table~\ref{tab:f2reach}). A dry-season delta rice field does not reach the
wilting point. The filter is therefore a \emph{safety} device that guarantees no
absurd state is accepted; it is not evidence of detection, and the layer must be
described as having two operative physical filters and one inert one.

\section{Device-Anchored Attestation}
\label{sec:anchor}

\subsection{The gateway record}

Proposition~\ref{prop:blind} shows that volume and depth are insufficient, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:coincide} shows that order is exactly what the adversary
manipulates. The defence must therefore observe timestamps --- and the only party
that can vouch for a timestamp is the device that acted on it.

Let the gateway hold a signed command record
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{G}=\{(t_j,u_j,\sigma_j)\},
\label{eq:gateway}
\end{equation}
where $\sigma_j$ is a signature over $(t_j,u_j)$ produced by the controller at
actuation time. The anchor test compares the claimed log against it:
\begin{equation}
A(L,\mathcal{G})=
\begin{cases}
\text{forge}, & \{t_i\}\neq\{t_j\}\ \vee\ \exists t:\ |u(t)-u_{\mathcal{G}}(t)|>\epsilon,\\
\text{match}, & \text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\label{eq:anchor}
\end{equation}
with $\epsilon=10^{-6}$\,mm, i.e.\ exact agreement up to floating-point noise.
Time-shifting, consolidation, addition, omission and depth inflation all alter
either the hour set or a depth at a common hour, so all are caught.

\begin{proposition}[Completeness of the anchor]
\label{prop:complete}
If a claimed log $L$ differs from the gateway record $\mathcal{G}$ in the
multiset of hours or in any depth at a shared hour, then $A(L,\mathcal{G})$
returns \text{forge}. If $L$ and $\mathcal{G}$ agree on both, then $L$ is a
faithful transcript and no forgery of the three quantities in \eqref{eq:three}
has occurred.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The first clause is the definition of \eqref{eq:anchor}. For the second, total
volume, individual depths and timestamps are all functions of the hour set and
the per-hour depths, so agreement on those two fixes all three.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Why this does not re-introduce false accusation}

Proposition~\ref{prop:complete} is strong enough to look dangerous: any
discrepancy is treated as forgery, including the forgetting and rounding that
Sec.~\ref{sec:guarantee} showed to be ubiquitous. The resolution is that the
anchor does not compare a log against a \emph{model}; it compares a transcript
against the \emph{source}. An honest farmer who forgets an entry is not accused
of fraud, because the system of record is the gateway, not the farmer's memory:
the claim is simply completed from $\mathcal{G}$. In operational terms the anchor
turns the log from \emph{evidence the farmer must get right} into \emph{a view of
a record the device already holds}. False accusation then requires the gateway to
disagree with itself, which is a device-integrity question rather than a
behavioural one, and is why the guarantee in Table~\ref{tab:falseacc} holds at
$0/60$ while the behavioural tiers hold at $26.7\%$ and $5.0\%$.

\subsection{Cost and the scope of the claim}

The anchor requires the gateway of the companion work~\cite{twingate2026}, which
actuates the pump and already writes a signed, time-stamped command record as
part of its safety function. The marginal cost of attestation is therefore the
storage and transmission of a record that exists regardless, which is why the
attested-log row of Table~\ref{tab:baseline} remains near zero.

The scope must nonetheless be stated precisely, because it is narrower than the
premise we started from. A log written by hand on a handset, with no device in
the loop, is \emph{not} admissible evidence on its own: Sec.~\ref{sec:results}
measures a rational adversary inflating credits by $69\%$ while escaping every
physics-based and volume-based test. The honest formulation of the result is that
near-zero-cost MRV is achievable, but only where a controller stands between the
farmer and the pump. Where irrigation is manual --- a sluice gate opened by hand
--- the anchor is unavailable and this layer does not apply.

\section{Subsidence Invariance}
\label{sec:subsidence}

AWD compliance is usually stated as an absolute water-table or ponding depth,
for example $15$\,cm below the surface, the reflooding threshold of the Safe-AWD
treatment in~\cite{balaine2019}. In a subsiding delta
the surface elevation $z_s(t)$ falls at rate $\dot z$, so an absolute threshold
measured against a fixed benchmark drifts.

\begin{proposition}[Drift of an absolute threshold]
\label{prop:subsid}
Over a crediting period of $T_c$ years an absolute threshold expressed against
a fixed datum shifts by $\dot z\,T_c$. For $\dot z\in[1.1,2.5]$\,cm/yr and
$T_c=5$\,yr the shift is $5.5$--$12.5$\,cm, that is $37$--$83\%$ of a
$15$\,cm threshold. A criterion written in the depletion fraction
\eqref{eq:depl} has zero drift, because $FC$ and $WP$ are soil properties
referenced to the root zone and not to an absolute elevation.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The absolute criterion compares a measured depth to a constant benchmark; if
the benchmark and the surface move apart at $\dot z$, the effective threshold
changes by $\dot z T_c$ over $T_c$ years. The depletion criterion compares
$FC-w_t$ to $(FC-WP)$, both of which are defined within the root zone and
translate rigidly with the surface, so their ratio is invariant to $z_s$.
\end{proof}

The subsidence rates are those measured for the delta by
\cite{minderhoud2017}. The practical consequence is that a scheme anchored on
depletion fraction needs no re-surveying of field benchmarks during a five-year
crediting period, whereas an absolute-depth scheme does.

\section{Cost Model and Break-Even}
\label{sec:cost}

Let a cooperative serve $A$ hectares with $N$ cropping seasons of device life,
and let the MRV cost per hectare per season decompose as
\begin{equation}
C=\underbrace{\frac{n_{\mathrm{dev}}\,p_{\mathrm{dev}}}{A\,N}}_{\text{hardware}}
+\underbrace{c_{\mathrm{conn}}}_{\text{connectivity}}
+\underbrace{h\,w}_{\text{labour}}
+\underbrace{\frac{c_{\mathrm{audit}}}{A}}_{\text{verification}},
\label{eq:cost}
\end{equation}
where $n_{\mathrm{dev}}$ and $p_{\mathrm{dev}}$ are the number and unit price of
devices, $h$ is the labour hours per hectare per season and $w$ the rural wage.
Revenue per hectare is
\begin{equation}
R=\kappa\,\rho ,
\label{eq:revenue}
\end{equation}
with $\kappa$ credits issued per hectare and $\rho$ the price per credit. The
scheme is economically viable when
\begin{equation}
C\ \le\ \beta R ,\qquad \beta\in(0,1),
\label{eq:breakeven}
\end{equation}
and $\beta=0.5$ is the level at which monitoring consumes half of the credit
revenue. Substituting \eqref{eq:revenue} gives the break-even issuance
\begin{equation}
\kappa_{\min}=\frac{C}{\beta\rho}.
\label{eq:kappamin}
\end{equation}

The tiers differ in $n_{\mathrm{dev}}$ and, more importantly, in which of the
three forgeable quantities of \eqref{eq:three} they can observe. Tier~0 is the
physics layer: $n_{\mathrm{dev}}=0$ beyond the controller, the log is written on
a handset the farmer already owns, and the filters of Sec.~\ref{sec:filters} run
on public weather data at no marginal cost. Tier~1 adds one flow meter at the
cooperative pumping station --- $n_{\mathrm{dev}}=1$ for the whole area $A$
rather than one per plot --- which observes the total volume. Tier~2 adds one
water-level node per cooperative, re-anchoring the twin and restoring the
calibration property a measurement-based MRV needs anyway.

None of the three observes timestamps, and by Prop.~\ref{prop:blind} the first
two are exactly invariant under the transformation that pays. The device anchor
of Sec.~\ref{sec:anchor} is therefore not an optional fourth tier but a
precondition of admissibility: it is produced by the same controller that
actuates the pump, so it adds storage and transmission of a record that already
exists, and no additional $n_{\mathrm{dev}}$.

Table~\ref{tab:baseline} compares the scheme with the three architectures it
replaces. With
$\kappa\in[4,6]$ and $\rho\in[\$15,\$25]$~\cite{rmi2025rice,upstream2026price},
revenue is $R\in[\$60,\$150]$ per hectare per season, so
$\kappa_{\min}$ at $\beta=0.5$ corresponds to a monitoring budget of
$\$30$--$75$ per hectare. The full stack sits an order of magnitude below that
ceiling, and the incremental cost of the anchor over a controller that is already
installed is about $1\%$ of conservative revenue. Table~\ref{tab:baseline}
decomposes the comparison against the three architectures it replaces; the
connectivity term is shown separately because it is the one that distinguishes a
sensor-per-plot design from a single shared gateway.

\begin{remark}
The device prices in Table~\ref{tab:baseline} are planning estimates, not
quotations. The ordering of the tiers and the break-even inequality
\eqref{eq:breakeven} do not depend on them; only the absolute margin does. A
quotation should replace the estimates before any procurement decision.
\end{remark}

\section{Experimental Design}
\label{sec:design}

The design is a comparison of four defence layers against nine log types, on a
field that deliberately violates every assumption of the twin.

\subsection{Removing the circularity}

The prior evaluation generated ``real'' trajectories with the default
\texttt{SoilProfile}, noiseless ERA5 forcing, irrigation as a one-hour impulse of
exactly $\{20,40\}$\,mm, a threshold of exactly $0.80$, and a farmer who records
the right depth at the right hour. Its $0/60$ false accusations were therefore
measurements of self-consistency. Here the field is generated by a separate
process that differs from the twin along seven axes, each with a published source
(Table~\ref{tab:setup}): soil texture drawn from the five FAO-56 Table 2
classes present in the delta; $K_c$ staged per Table 12 rather than fixed at
$1.05$; irrigation efficiency $\eta\in[0.60,0.85]$, so the farmer reports water
\emph{pumped} while only $\eta u$ reaches the root zone; field rainfall drawn as
$0.7$--$1.4$ times the grid cell plus Gaussian noise; bund seepage
$0$--$0.02$\,mm\,h$^{-1}$; recording behaviour with $5\%$ forgetting, rounding to
$5$\,mm, a $35\%$ chance of a one-day delay and $15\%$ depth error within
$\pm35\%$; and an initial depletion $w_0$ drawn from $[0,0.45]$ rather than
assumed to be field capacity. Rounding and delay are applied per \emph{event},
not per hour: applying a $5$\,mm rounding to a $40$\,mm event spread over eight
hours rounds each $5$\,mm fragment to zero and silently deletes the record, an
artefact that produced empty logs in an early version of this harness.

Weather is hourly ERA5 from the Open-Meteo archive for Can Tho, Soc Trang and Ca
Mau over 2024 (water-scarce) and 2025 (water-surplus), no missing values, window
1 March to 29 May ($2\,160$\,h), the dry season in which AWD is practised.

\subsection{Four defence layers}

Layer~1 is the published point-model detector: replay under the default profile,
absolute pump-gap threshold $\tau_0=5$\,mm. Layer~2 is the robust detector of
\eqref{eq:robust} with the proportional threshold of \eqref{eq:tau}, run both on
a reduced $\Theta$ ($12$ configurations $\times2$ initial states $=24$ replays
per log, used for the $10$-seed runs) and on the full $\Theta$ ($30\times5=150$
replays, used as a cross-check). Layer~3 is the device anchor of \eqref{eq:anchor}.
Layer~4, for reference, is physics alone with no volume measurement.

\subsection{Nine log types, four of them adversarial}

Three are the naive forgeries of the prior work: added events on rainy days,
one third of entries inflated to the whole storage span, and a $96$\,h window of
claims deleted. Four are adversarial and, unlike the naive ones, are constructed
by an optimiser rather than a fixed recipe: consolidation onto a $24$\,h and a
$96$\,h grid; a rational adversary that selects whichever consolidation window
maximises $n_{\mathrm{dry}}$ subject to preserving the total and respecting
\eqref{eq:v5}; and the time-shifting hill-climb of Sec.~\ref{sec:timeshift},
which searches integer offsets in $[-36,36]$ and accepts a move only if the total
is preserved bit-for-bit, every depth stays below the infiltration bound, and
$n_{\mathrm{dry}}$ strictly increases.

Two harness defects had to be fixed before these numbers meant anything, and both
are recorded in the source because they are easy to reintroduce. First, a fraud
generator that finds no improving move returns the \emph{original} honest log, so
an unconditional count of ``attacks caught'' silently includes cases where no
attack occurred; every adversarial row therefore carries a \texttt{changed} flag
and all attack statistics are conditioned on it. Second, comparisons must use the
baseline of the variant itself: the paper-condition honest log is generated by a
separate call, and scoring it against the field-condition record of a different
soil realisation falsely ``caught'' $56$ of $60$ honest logs.

\subsection{Metrics}

For each layer we report the escape rate, i.e.\ the fraction of forged logs
neither tier flags, and the false-accusation rate on honest logs. For credit
impact we report $n_{\mathrm{dry}}$ of \eqref{eq:ndry}, the number of
credit-eligible dry phases, against the honest field baseline. A configuration
that maximises claimed volume is not automatically profitable: an early adversary
that inflated claims by $344$\,mm drove $n_{\mathrm{dry}}$ to zero and would have
earned nothing, so volume is reported only as a diagnostic.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{The published detector is unsafe, not merely optimistic}

Table~\ref{tab:falseacc} gives the central negative result. On logs generated
under paper conditions the point-model detector accuses nobody, reproducing the
prior $0/60$. On the same detector applied to field-condition logs it accuses
$26.7\%$ of honest farmers at tier~1. The cause is \eqref{eq:gap} with an
absolute threshold: forgetting one entry, rounding, or recording a day late all
produce $G>5$\,mm without any wrongdoing, and the measured gap on honest logs
reached $75$\,mm. The proportional threshold of \eqref{eq:tau} reduces this to
$5.0\%$, and the device anchor to $0/60$.

\subsection{Physics cannot see the attack that pays}

Table~\ref{tab:credit} is the second negative result, and it is the one that
matters economically. Relative to an honest field baseline of $3.23$ creditable
dry phases per season, time-shifting raises the count to $5.48$, an inflation of
$69\%$; under the full uncertainty set the figure is $6.14$ against $3.44$, or
$78\%$. Consolidation reaches $+52\%$ and $+53\%$, and the rational adversary
$+63\%$ and $+70\%$. Table~\ref{tab:defence} shows the corresponding escape
rates: time-shifting escapes both the point model and the robust physics layer
in $91\%$ of cases, and the rational adversary in $100\%$, exactly as
Proposition~\ref{prop:coincide} predicts. The infiltration filter $V_5$ fires
$648$ times across the evaluation but exclusively on the crude
depth-inflation forgery, never on a time-preserving-volume attack --- it is
useful, and orthogonal to the threat that pays.

\subsection{The anchor closes every class}

Table~\ref{tab:defence} also gives the constructive half. Of $281$ audited logs,
$116$ were genuinely altered by an attack; the device anchor caught $116/116$ and
accused none of $60$ honest field-condition logs. Under the full $\Theta$ the
figures are $37/37$ and $0/18$. Naive forgeries are caught by every layer; the
distinction is entirely in the adversarial rows, where physics and volume both
fail and only the anchor holds.

\subsection{The cost of robustness is measurable}

Replacing the point model by the intersection over $\Theta$ is not free, and the
loss must be reported. On omission fraud the point model catches $78.3\%$ of
altered logs while the robust rule catches $40\%$ --- the same trade-off direction
as the proportional threshold, and for the same reason: a defence that refuses to
accuse on the strength of one model configuration also refuses some true
positives. Naive forgeries remain at $100\%$ under both. We regard this as the
correct side of the trade, since a false accusation falls on a farmer with no
means of appeal while a missed forgery falls on a verification body that samples
anyway, but it is a loss of detection power and not a Pareto improvement.

\subsection{Reduced and full uncertainty sets agree}

Every conclusion above holds under both the reduced $\Theta$ ($24$ replays per
log, $281$ logs, $10$ seeds) and the full $\Theta$ ($150$ replays, $85$ logs,
$3$ seeds): false accusation $0/60$ and $0/18$, anchor completeness $116/116$ and
$37/37$, time-shift escape $91\%$ and $100\%$, credit inflation $+69\%$ and
$+78\%$. The reduced set was used for the large runs because it contains the
extremes of every axis, which is where an innocent world is most likely to exist;
the full set confirms that no interior configuration changes a verdict.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What survives of the original claim}

The premise that motivated this work was attractive: an irrigation command is
already a digital event, so MRV evidence should be nearly free. That premise
survives, but not in the form we first published. It survives because the
controller that issues the command can also sign it, and the signed record is
what makes the evidence admissible --- not because a hydrological model can
detect a lie. Where the original draft claimed that physics attests the log, the
correct statement is that physics \emph{constrains} the log: it rules out depths
no soil can hold and states no field can reach, which is worth having, and it is
silent about the one transformation that pays.

\subsection{Why publishing the threshold is not the problem}

One might conclude that $\varepsilon$ should be kept secret. It should not, for
two reasons. A verifier must be able to reproduce a decision, which requires
published parameters. And secrecy would not help: by Proposition~\ref{prop:blind}
the time-shifting attack preserves the total volume and every individual depth,
so it does not need to know $\varepsilon$ in order to stay beneath the
infiltration bound --- it needs only to avoid moving water, which is visible to
any farmer. The vulnerability is structural, not a consequence of disclosure.

\subsection{Relation to remote sensing}

Satellite radar detects irrigation events but revisits every two to four days and
cannot resolve a $72$\,h dry phase in mid-period, which is the crediting unit.
It therefore cannot substitute for a command record, and it cannot detect
time-shifting within a revisit interval either. Its role remains the one VM0051
Appendix 4 assigns it: a per-plot cross-check that lowers the frequency of field
verification~\cite{vm0051}.

\subsection{Limitations}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Simulated farmers.} Forgeries are injected into simulated reference
trajectories. A field pilot is needed to measure behavioural response, in
particular whether farmers shift their \emph{actual} pumping once the anchor is
known to exist --- an attack our model cannot represent, since it changes $M$
rather than $L$.
\item \emph{The anchor assumes an honest gateway.}
Proposition~\ref{prop:complete} is completeness against log forgery, not against
a compromised controller. Device integrity, key custody and replay protection
are assumed from the companion work and are not re-established here.
\item \emph{Not applicable to manual irrigation.} Where a sluice is opened by
hand there is no command record, so the anchor is unavailable. This excludes a
substantial share of delta farms and bounds the addressable population of the
scheme.
\item \emph{$\Theta$ is a judgement.} Five textures, two $K_c$ regimes, three
efficiencies and five initial states bracket the delta but do not exhaust it. A
field outside $\Theta$ loses the guarantee of Proposition~\ref{prop:noalarm},
which is exactly the situation the proportional threshold and the query verdict
exist to absorb.
\item \emph{Device prices are planning estimates, not quotations.} The tier
ordering and the break-even inequality are unaffected; the absolute margin is.
\item \emph{No emission quantification.} The layer produces activity data.
Coupling it to a methane model and locally calibrated emission factors is
separate work, as is the cost table's assumption that credits scale with
$n_{\mathrm{dry}}$.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}

We set out to make rice MRV nearly free by auditing a farmer-written irrigation
log against soil-water physics, and the first version of that layer reported no
false accusation and complete detection. Both numbers were artefacts of a field
generated by the auditing model itself. Rebuilding the field along seven axes of
realism, and replacing fixed forgery recipes with an adversary that optimises the
credited quantity, changes the conclusion.

The point-model detector falsely accuses $26.7\%$ of honest farmers, because an
absolute pump-gap threshold cannot distinguish a forgotten entry from a secret
irrigation. A proportional threshold and an intersection over a $150$-element
uncertainty set reduce that to $5.0\%$ and $0/60$ respectively, at a measured
cost in detection of omission fraud, from $78.3\%$ to $40\%$. Meanwhile a
volume-preserving, depth-bounded permutation of claimed times inflates creditable
dry phases by $69\%$ to $78\%$ while escaping both physical tiers in $91\%$ to
$100\%$ of cases, and we prove this is unavoidable for any detector whose verdict
is a function of the replayed trajectory: delaying a claim both lengthens the dry
phase and increases the deficit available to it, so profit and undetectability
share a gradient.

The repair is not hydrological. Matching the claimed log against the gateway's
signed, time-stamped command record catches $116/116$ genuinely forged logs and
accuses none of $60$ honest ones, and it costs nothing beyond a controller the
companion work already deploys. The honest formulation of the result is
therefore narrower and more useful than the one we started with: near-zero-cost
MRV for low-emission rice is achievable, but only where a device stands between
the farmer and the pump. Three previously published numbers are corrected
accordingly --- the admissible depth is the range $[49.0,86.0]$\,mm rather than a
single $61$\,mm; the third physical filter cannot activate on dry-season delta
rice, clipped depletion peaking at $0.9987$ over $150$ configurations; and a
subthreshold-based absolute gap test is inadmissible. Code, both uncertainty
sets, all $281$ audited logs and $46$ unit tests, one per design hole, are
public.
